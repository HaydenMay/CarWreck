# Milestone 1: first playable driving prototype

Playtest link (WebGL, GitHub Pages, HTTPS): **https://haydenmay.github.io/CarWreck/**
Source branch: `feature/playable-driving-prototype` (not merged to `main`). The published files live on the
`gh-pages` branch, which holds build output only.

## Controls

| | Desktop | Touch (phones / tablets, landscape) | Gamepad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accelerate | W / Up | GAS (bottom right) | Right trigger / A |
| Brake, then reverse | S / Down / Space | BRAKE REVERSE (left of GAS) | Left trigger / B |
| Steer | A D / Left Right | Left and right arrows (bottom left) | Left stick / D-pad |
| Reset car, damage and obstacles | R | RESET (top right) | Y or Start |

Touch controls appear automatically on phones and tablets (and on the first real touch on any device). The
**TOUCH: ON/OFF** button (top right) forces them. Buttons are independent pointers: steering and pedals can be held
together. Portrait shows a "rotate your device" prompt. The page blocks scrolling, pinch-zoom and the long-press menu.

## What is in the scene

`Assets/Driving/Scenes/DriveArena.unity` (generated by `CarWreck > Build Driving Scene`): 154 m square arena, perimeter
walls, a hazard-striped crash wall 70 m ahead of the start pad, staggered pillars, angled concrete barriers, three crate
pyramids (dynamic), three 900 kg blocks (dynamic) and a gentle ramp. The muscle car is built by the existing `VehicleRig`
exactly as in the Collision Lab and keeps its `CollisionDamageReceiver` / `VehicleDamageModel` / `VehicleHitbox`.

**Why obstacles are kinematic Rigidbodies:** `CollisionDamageReceiver` ignores contacts with anything that has no
Rigidbody (it treats those as "the ground"). So every wall, barrier and pillar is a *kinematic* Rigidbody (immovable, infinite
mass), and crates/blocks are dynamic. Static colliders without a Rigidbody (ground, ramp) never dent the car. No change to the
deformation engine was needed. The only configuration change: `receiver.oneImpactPerLaunch = false` at runtime (the lab arms
each impactor once per launch; in driving every distinct hit counts, with the receiver's 0.5 s per-body cooldown).

## Driving model (`ArcadeCarController`)

One Rigidbody, no wheel colliders. The existing box shell rests on the ground with a low-friction physics material; the
script applies, all in `ForceMode.Acceleration` (m/s², independent of mass), only while 3+ of 4 downward probes touch ground
(fewer = proportionally less traction, none = no control in the air):

* **Drive:** acceleration falls off as `1 - (v/vmax)^3`. Brake is a fixed deceleration; below 0.5 m/s the brake pedal becomes reverse.
* **Steering:** target yaw rate, from 100 deg/s at low speed to 34 deg/s at max speed, fading out below 3 m/s (no spinning on
  the spot) and mirrored when reversing; the body is torqued towards it. The input ramps in/out so a key tap is a gentle correction.
* **Grip:** sideways velocity is removed at `grip` per second, capped at 26 m/s². Past the cap, or when braking hard while
  steering (grip x 0.55), the car slides.
* **Stability:** low centre of mass (from `VehicleRig`), a leveling torque and roll/pitch damping while grounded, and
  speed-proportional downforce. A big hit can still flip the car; "Flipped? Press R" appears after a moment.
* **Collisions:** plain Unity physics (ContinuousDynamic). Damage comes only from the existing receiver.

## Chase camera (`ChaseCamera`)

Follows the car's yaw through a critically damped filter (leaning slightly towards the travel direction), locked to the car
position with smoothed height, FOV 58 to 70 with speed, no shake. Fixed obstacles between car and camera pull it in and up.
After a crash, once the car has nearly stopped, the camera swings to the struck side (a side not blocked by a wall) until the
player drives on; without this you could never see front damage from behind.

## Inspector tuning

`ArcadeCarController` (on the car): `acceleration` 13, `maxSpeed` 30 (m/s), `reverseAcceleration` 7, `reverseMaxSpeed` 10,
`brakeDeceleration` 26, `coastDeceleration` 1.2, `steerRateLow` 100, `steerRateHigh` 34, `steerFullAuthoritySpeed` 3,
`steerResponse` 28, `steerRise` 5, `steerReturn` 9, `grip` 9, `maxGripAcceleration` 26, `brakingGripScale` 0.55, `downforce` 0.22,
`levelingStrength` 10, `levelingDamping` 3. `ChaseCamera`: `distance` 7.5, `height` 3.1, `lookHeight` 1.0, `yawSmoothTime`
0.28, `velocityBlend` 0.35, FOV, obstruction. `DrivingGame`: `hitchMilliseconds` 40 (impacts slower than this are flagged).
Edit defaults in the scripts, then re-run **CarWreck > Build Driving Scene** (serialized scene values override script defaults).

## Build, validate, publish

```
# scene (re-run after changing script defaults) and automated simulation checks
Unity -batchmode -nographics -quit -projectPath . -executeMethod CarWreck.Driving.EditorTools.DrivingSceneBuilder.Build
Unity -batchmode -nographics -quit -projectPath . -executeMethod CarWreck.Driving.EditorTools.DrivingValidation.Run
# WebGL player -> Builds/WebGLDrive (page template: Assets/WebGLTemplates/CarWreck)
Unity -batchmode -quit -projectPath . -buildTarget WebGL -executeMethod CarWreck.Driving.EditorTools.DrivingWebBuild.Build
# publish to gh-pages (GitHub Pages, source branch gh-pages, path /)
bash Docs/Driving/publish-preview.sh
```

The page exposes the running build id as `window.carwreckBuild` (also shown on the loading screen) and the game state as
`window.carwreckState` (speed, position, input sources, impacts, worst deformation time, fps) for diagnostics.
`version.json` on the site records the source commit.

## Known limitations

* **Impact hitches:** the existing deformation runs synchronously on the main thread. Measured per-hit deformation time:
  14-39 ms in desktop Chromium WebGL for 25-88 km/h hits, 80-100 ms in the Unity editor. The Collision Lab's 157 ms hitch was
  not reproduced here; phones will be slower. No optimization was attempted (out of scope; separate research).
* Wheels do not spin or steer visually (they are rigid parts of the model); no tyre marks, audio or damage-based handling.
* Only the muscle car; no ground/ramp damage; heavy hits can leave crate stacks and the car in awkward poses (press R).
* Initial load: about 16 MB (gzip, unpacked in the page), then 10 to 20 s of startup on desktop; mobile browsers untested on hardware.
* Unity Personal splash ("Made with Unity") shows briefly at start.
