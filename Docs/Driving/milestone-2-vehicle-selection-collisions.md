# Milestone 2: vehicle selection, distinct handling, vehicle-to-vehicle collisions

Playtest link (WebGL, GitHub Pages, HTTPS): **https://haydenmay.github.io/CarWreck/**
Branch `feature/vehicle-selection-collisions` (built on `feature/playable-driving-prototype`; neither is merged to `main`).
Milestone 1 notes: [milestone-1-driving-prototype.md](milestone-1-driving-prototype.md).

## Using it

* The game opens on the **menu**: choose your car (three cards, live 3D preview that slowly orbits the chosen car), choose the
  **target vehicle** (parked car to crash into; the preview switches to it), press **DRIVE**. Everything starts reset.
* **CHANGE CAR** (top right, left of TOUCH) or **M / Esc / Tab** (gamepad: Select) reopens the menu at any time; the game is paused
  while it is open. DRIVE resets the arena, both vehicles and all damage.
* **RESET** / **R** / gamepad Y restores both vehicles (pose, velocity, all damage) and the movable obstacles.
* Controls otherwise as Milestone 1: W/S/A/D (or arrows), touch buttons (gas and brake right, steering left), gamepad sticks/triggers.

## Reverse: what was wrong

Milestone 1 reverse accelerated at 7 m/s² towards a 10 m/s cap. Three things combined: (1) the cap was never reached, because the
car's passive drag (hull friction on the ground, Rigidbody drag, downforce friction, about 2-3 m/s²) cancels most of the thrust near
the cap, so the real terminal reverse speed was ~8 m/s (29 km/h); (2) low reverse acceleration meant 20-25 km/h after the 2-3 s of
room that arena distances typically allow; (3) the same drag held forward speed (97 km/h vs 108). The smallest fix: new
`driveResistance` (3 m/s²) added to the drive force in both directions so the caps are close to real top speeds, reverse acceleration
11 m/s² (was 7), reverse cap 13.5 m/s. Muscle reverse now reaches 30 km/h in 0.8 s and 44 km/h terminal (editor sim), 32 km/h after 1 s and
43 km/h after 2 s in desktop WebGL. Braking-to-reverse and mirrored reverse steering are unchanged. No rear-damage multiplier exists:
reversing into a wall at 43 km/h gave 81% strength from the existing energy model; a rear hit into a parked pickup at 44 km/h gave 56%.

## Vehicle-to-vehicle damage

The existing `CollisionDamageReceiver` supports this directly: it ignores only contacts with bodies that have no Rigidbody (ground),
treats another dynamic Rigidbody as an impactor with its real mass (reduced mass in the energy), and uses that body's box collider
as the impact footprint. Each vehicle has its own receiver, so both are damaged from the same Rigidbody collision event, at the
contact area, with no engine change. Only configuration: `oneImpactPerLaunch = false` (the lab arms each impactor once per launch).
No code in `DamageLab/` changed.

* Player and target are both built at runtime by `VehicleRig` from the three existing definitions (so vehicles can be swapped without reloading).
* The target starts at rest 42 m ahead of the start pad, broadside (yaw 90), with open ground around it (the pillars were moved out of
  the middle). It is a normal dynamic Rigidbody with a higher-friction ground material (it slides and stops like a car with its brakes on)
  and no controller or AI. It is not frozen.
* One contact gives one damage record per vehicle (extra receiver events during the push were below the damage threshold); a second
  distinct hit after backing off adds a second record to each; damage persists; reset restores both exactly.

## Known limitations

* Player and target look the same when they are the same model (same paint); no marker.
* Wheels still do not spin or steer; no audio.
* The parked target has no brakes model beyond ground friction; a hard hit can tumble it.
* The pickup's 1,350 kg is close to the muscle car's 1,400 kg, so pickup-vs-muscle differences come mostly from geometry and handling.
* Engine thrust keeps pushing while the player holds gas against the target (by design: no contact detection of "stuck").
* Mobile hardware performance and iOS Safari WebGL limits are untested; hitches on phones may be larger than desktop.
* First visit downloads about 22 MB; startup 10-20 s on desktop.

## Publishing

`bash Docs/Driving/publish-preview.sh` renames the Unity files to include the build id and keeps the previous build's files for one
more publish, so a browser holding an older cached `index.html` still works; the page also compares its build id with `version.json`
and reloads once if it is stale. The loading screen and `window.carwreckBuild` show the build id.

## Damage tuning after the first playtest (build 20261016-0139)

Feedback: the player seemed to take the brunt, and target damage did not grow with how hard it was hit. Measured (editor sim, same collision, both cars):

* **Both cars always get the same strength** (the receiver gives each the same reduced-mass collision energy), so equal damage per hit is by design.
* **It saturated.** The receiver default (`energyScale` 60,000 J) reaches about 90% strength at 70 km/h closing speed, so 70 km/h and 100 km/h looked the same.
* **Side hits look smaller on the target.** In a T-bone the player's front dents 2-5x more vertices (a wide crush) than the struck door (a narrow patch), at the same strength and 0.3-0.45 m depth. This is the engine's side-region behaviour (a damage-profile property shared with the Collision Lab); it was left alone and needs approval to change.

Change (configuration only, no engine code): `DrivingGame.damageEnergyScale` = 100,000 J (applied to both receivers at spawn), so strength now rises across the speed range. Desktop WebGL, muscle into parked muscle: 50 km/h gives 48%, 62 km/h gives 64%, 86 km/h gives 86%, on both cars. Reverse rear hit at 44 km/h into a pickup now reads about 39% (was 56%). The parked target's ground friction was lowered (0.5 to 0.35) so it slides farther: 3.3 m, 8 m, 11.7 m for those three hits.

## Free look (build 20261016-0210)

Swipe (touch) or drag (mouse) on empty screen to orbit the camera around the car; vertical movement raises/lowers it. The view eases back behind the car 3 s after the last swipe, and a swipe cancels the post-crash damage view. In the menu a drag spins the car preview. `LookDragPad` is an invisible full-screen layer behind all buttons, so pedal/steering fingers never reach it; verified with a synthetic two-finger test (gas held while swiping). Not tested on a real device.

## Energy split between vehicles (build 20261016-0219)

The receiver computed the energy a collision dissipates and then gave all of it to each car, so two-car hits applied twice the dissipated energy (walls were right: the wall absorbs nothing). `CollisionDamageReceiver` now has an opt-in `shareEnergyWithVehicles` (default **off**, so the Collision Lab and its validation are unchanged) with `sharedEnergyFraction` (default 0.5). When on and the other body also has a receiver, this car applies only that fraction. `DrivingGame.shareEnergyBetweenVehicles` turns it on; `damageEnergyScale` was lowered from 100,000 to 80,000 J to compensate. One two-line change in the shared receiver, no deformation code touched.

Desktop WebGL, muscle car into parked pickup: 55 km/h gives 38%, 65 km/h gives 49%, 86 km/h gives 70% (both cars); reverse into a wall at 43 km/h gives 71% (walls unaffected by the split). Editor sim at 72 km/h closing: 43-58% depending on the pair; a 44 km/h reverse hit into a pickup gives 22-26%. Equal stiffness is assumed (0.5 each); making one vehicle softer or stiffer than another is a later per-vehicle setting. Collision Lab validation re-run: unchanged results. Driving validation: 34/34.

## Per-vehicle softness (build 20261016-0254)

`CollisionDamageReceiver.softness` (default 1, only used when `shareEnergyWithVehicles` is on) replaces the fixed 0.5 split: a car absorbs `softness / (softness + other.softness)` of the dissipated energy, so the shares always sum to 100%. Each vehicle's value comes from its `DriveProfile.damageSoftness` (driving layer only; the `VehicleDefinition` and damage-profile assets are untouched). Starting values, not balance: **hatchback 1.25, muscle car 1.0, pickup 0.8**, matching their descriptions (light and easily hurt, baseline, sturdy). Examples: hatchback vs pickup 61% / 39%; muscle vs hatchback 44% / 56%; same-vs-same 50% / 50%.

Checked: driving validation 43/43 (new: in all nine pairings the softer car takes the stronger hit, equal cars are equal); Collision Lab validation unchanged. Live desktop WebGL at about 74 km/h closing: hatchback into pickup 59% (hatchback) / 43% (pickup); pickup into hatchback 42% / 57%; muscle into muscle 60% / 60%.
